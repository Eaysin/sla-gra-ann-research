# Literature Review: Multi-Response Optimization and Prediction of SLA 3D Printing Parameters
1. **Kadauw, A. (2025). *Neural Network Optimization of Mechanical Properties of ABS-like Photopolymer Utilizing Stereolithography (SLA) 3D Printing*. Journal of Manufacturing and Materials Processing, 9(4), 116.**
   This study investigates how SLA process parameters—specifically build orientation, lifting speed, lifting distance, and exposure time—affect the tensile strength, yield strength, hardness, and surface roughness of ABS-like photopolymers. The author trains four feedforward Artificial Neural Network (ANN) models on 54 experimental builds, achieving correlation coefficients above 0.98 across all measured properties. Combining ANN predictions with multi-objective optimization successfully identifies parameter combinations that maximize mechanical strength while minimizing surface roughness.
2. **Martín-Montal, J., Pernas-Sánchez, J., & Varas, D. (2021). *Experimental Characterization Framework for SLA Additive Manufacturing Materials*. Polymers, 13(7), 1147.**
   The authors present a standardized experimental testing framework to evaluate the mechanical behavior of SLA-printed polymers under different printing angles (0°–90°), layer thicknesses (50 and 100 µm), and post-curing durations. Their tensile and compression experiments demonstrate that layer thickness primarily affects small-strain elastic responses, whereas post-curing time is critical for achieving optimal overall mechanical strength.
3. **Hu, G., Cao, Z., Hopkins, M., et al. (2019). *Optimizing the Hardness of SLA Printed Objects by Using the Neural Network and Genetic Algorithm*. Procedia Manufacturing, 38, 117–124.**
   This research integrates an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) with a Genetic Algorithm (GA) to model and optimize the surface hardness of SLA-fabricated components across varying build orientations (0°–90°), UV post-curing times, and thermal annealing durations. Over 100 experimental samples were tested, revealing that build orientation exerts the strongest influence on final part hardness and that hybrid ANN optimization significantly lowers prediction error compared to traditional trial-and-error approaches.
4. **Shanmugasundaram, S. A., Razmi, J., Mian, M. J., & Ladani, L. (2020). *Mechanical Anisotropy and Surface Roughness in Additively Manufactured Parts Fabricated by Stereolithography (SLA) Using Statistical Analysis*. Materials, 13(11), 2496.**
   Using Taguchi Design of Experiments (DoE) and Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), this paper examines mechanical anisotropy and surface roughness in SLA components printed across multiple build and angular orientations according to ASTM D638 standards. The findings show that while SLA parts exhibit lower anisotropy than filament-based 3D prints, post-print aging and curing conditions substantially improve ultimate tensile strength.
5. **Hamdi, A., & Merghache, S. M. (2023). *Application of Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) and Grey Relational Analysis (GRA) to Modeling and Optimization of the Material Ratio Curve Parameters When Turning Hard Steel*. International Journal of Advanced Manufacturing Technology.**
   This study demonstrates the effectiveness of pairing Grey Relational Analysis (GRA) with Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) to solve complex, nonlinear multi-response manufacturing optimization problems. GRA consolidates multiple conflicting performance metrics into a single unified Grey Relational Grade (GRG), enabling the ANN model to accurately predict overall process performance and determine optimal operating parameters.
